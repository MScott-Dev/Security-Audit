# 🛡️ Botium Toys | Internal IT Security Audit & Risk Assessment

**Project Type:** Cybersecurity Portfolio Project  
**Focus Areas:** IT Auditing · Risk Assessment · Security Controls · Asset Management · Compliance  
**Framework:** NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)

---

## 📌 Project Overview

This project involves conducting an internal IT security audit and risk assessment for **Botium Toys**, a fictional U.S.-based retail company experiencing business growth through its online storefront and international customer base.

The objective was to evaluate the company's existing security posture, identify potential vulnerabilities, assess risks to critical business assets, and determine where security controls and compliance practices needed improvement.

Using the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF), I evaluated the company's infrastructure, employee devices, internal network, data storage, business systems, and existing security practices.

The assessment identified several security gaps involving access control, data protection, disaster recovery, password management, and regulatory compliance.

## 🎯 Audit Objectives

- Evaluate the organization's existing security posture.
- Identify and document critical business assets.
- Identify potential threats, risks, and vulnerabilities.
- Evaluate existing security controls and identify deficiencies.
- Assess potential business impacts resulting from asset loss or security incidents.
- Identify areas requiring improved regulatory compliance.
- Recommend security improvements to reduce organizational risk.

## ⚠️ Risk Assessment & Security Findings

### Overall Risk Rating

<details>
<summary>🔴 <strong>Risk Score: 8/10 — High</strong></summary>

The organization received a risk score of **8 out of 10**, indicating a relatively high level of risk.

This assessment reflects inadequate asset management, missing security controls, insufficient access restrictions, and potential gaps in regulatory compliance.

Without improvements, these deficiencies could expose sensitive customer information, disrupt business operations, and result in financial or regulatory consequences.

</details>

### Risk Description

<details>
<summary>⚠️ <strong>Identified Organizational Risks</strong></summary>

Botium Toys has inadequate asset management practices and does not have all the necessary security controls in place.

The organization may also be exposed to compliance issues involving U.S. and international regulations and standards.

</details>

### Control Best Practices

<details>
<summary>🛡️ <strong>NIST CSF — Identify Function</strong></summary>

The first of the five functions in the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) is **Identify**.

Botium Toys should dedicate resources to:

- Identifying and documenting organizational assets.
- Classifying existing assets according to their importance and sensitivity.
- Determining the potential impact of asset loss, including its effect on business continuity.
- Establishing asset management procedures.

</details>

### Detailed Security Findings

<details>
<summary>🔐 <strong>Access Control & Data Protection</strong></summary>

- All employees currently have access to internally stored data, potentially including cardholder data and customers' personally identifiable information (PII/SPII).
- Encryption is not currently used to protect customers' credit card information accepted, processed, transmitted, and stored in the company's internal database.
- Least privilege and separation of duties have not been implemented.

**Primary Concern:** Unauthorized access to sensitive information and increased exposure of customer data.

</details>

<details>
<summary>🟢 <strong>Existing Security Controls</strong></summary>

The audit identified several security controls already implemented:

- Availability and data integrity controls are in place.
- A firewall blocks network traffic based on defined security rules.
- Antivirus software is installed and regularly monitored by the IT department.
- Physical security includes adequate locks and updated CCTV surveillance.
- Fire detection and prevention systems are operational.
- Privacy policies, procedures, and processes have been established and are enforced.

**Positive Finding:** The organization has established foundational security measures that can be strengthened through additional controls and consistent monitoring.

</details>

<details>
<summary>🚨 <strong>Security Gaps & Vulnerabilities</strong></summary>

The following deficiencies were identified:

- **Intrusion Detection:** No intrusion detection system (IDS) has been installed.
- **Disaster Recovery:** No disaster recovery plan currently exists.
- **Data Backups:** Critical business data is not backed up.
- **Password Security:** Existing password requirements are insufficient and do not align with current security recommendations.
- **Password Management:** No centralized password management system exists to enforce password requirements.
- **Legacy Systems:** Legacy systems are monitored and maintained, but there is no established maintenance schedule or clearly defined intervention process.

**Primary Concern:** These deficiencies increase the organization's exposure to security incidents, data loss, unauthorized access, and operational downtime.

</details>

<details>
<summary>🌎 <strong>Regulatory Compliance & Privacy</strong></summary>

- The IT department has established a plan to notify E.U. customers within 72 hours of a security breach.
- Privacy policies, procedures, and processes have been developed and are enforced among IT department members and other employees.

**Compliance Considerations:**

- **GDPR:** General Data Protection Regulation requirements for protecting personal data and handling applicable data breaches involving E.U. individuals.
- **PCI DSS:** Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard requirements for protecting payment card information.
- **NIST CSF:** Cybersecurity framework for identifying, assessing, managing, and reducing organizational cybersecurity risks.

**Primary Concern:** Failure to implement appropriate security controls could expose the company to regulatory penalties, financial losses, and reputational damage.

</details>

## 🛠️ Recommended Security Improvements

Based on the identified findings, the following improvements would help strengthen Botium Toys' security posture.

| Priority | Recommendation | Security Benefit |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Critical | Implement least privilege access controls | Reduces unauthorized access |
| Critical | Encrypt sensitive customer and payment data | Protects data confidentiality |
| Critical | Establish regular data backups | Reduces risk of permanent data loss |
| High | Develop a disaster recovery plan | Improves business continuity |
| High | Implement centralized password management | Strengthens password enforcement |
| High | Review and improve password policies | Reduces password-related risks |
| High | Implement an intrusion detection system (IDS) | Improves threat detection |
| Medium | Establish a scheduled legacy system maintenance plan | Reduces operational vulnerabilities |
| Medium | Create and maintain an asset inventory | Improves asset visibility and accountability |

## 💡 Key Takeaways

This project reinforced the importance of identifying organizational assets, understanding security risks, and implementing appropriate controls to protect sensitive information.

One of the biggest takeaways was that having security tools in place, such as firewalls and antivirus software, does not necessarily mean an organization has a strong security posture.

Effective cybersecurity requires multiple layers of protection, including proper access controls, encryption, continuous monitoring, data backups, disaster recovery planning, and regulatory compliance.

**A strong security posture requires identifying risks before they become incidents.**
